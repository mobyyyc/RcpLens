# T05 follow-up: receipt wallet

The user's 2026-10-08 direction supersedes the earlier compact recent-three-card layout. This remains T05 work; T06/T07 are awaiting approval.

## Interaction and appearance

At the user's request, an empty wallet retains the previous tilted-paper/pocket illustration and centered welcome layout. Once receipts exist, the physical wallet stays at the bottom of the viewport. Only the papers scroll: swipe up pulls subsequent receipts out of the pocket; swipe down pushes them back in. Receipts run from oldest to newest down the screen; newer papers overlap older papers, with the wallet foremost. Every unarchived receipt belongs to the scrollable stack. The scroll surfaces extend behind the system bars and use native soft edge effects at the top and bottom of the screen. There is no central viewport mask or fade at the pocket entrance. Short stacks are lowered against the pocket: the newest paper remains tucked 32 points behind its front, whether the wallet contains one, two, three or many receipts. An opaque pocket backing conceals paper below the wallet without intercepting toolbar taps. Papers alternate subtle tilts; accessibility text sizes use straight, separated papers. At the end, native scroll rubber-banding moves the pocket and papers together before they settle. A jump-to-newest control appears for larger collections; All receipts provides month/store browsing without requiring named wallets.

The normal-size stack uses a continuous, monotone screen-space projection. Receipt positions respond fastest around the physical screen’s upper third, widening the space between papers so more of one receipt is readable. Travel slows toward the wallet, compressing neighboring papers to a small gap. The curve bounds consecutive gaps between 36 and 224 points (the widest actual gap is below the upper bound), preserves chronology, and reverses continuously with the swipe. Papers retain their size; it is the spacing and travel speed that vary. Native scrolling supplies inertia and rubber-banding. At the last receipt, the projection compensates for the end pull so that both wallet panels and the newest paper move together. Reduced Motion and accessibility text sizes retain the regular layout.

Only papers in the projected viewport plus a small margin are rendered. A logical scroll surface maintains the native content extent and jump-to-newest target; inverse projection determines the visible slice. Each render captures an immutable receipt snapshot so resetting, archiving or deleting records cannot invalidate its indices. Preview frames capture the projected positions and remain frozen while a paper is expanded, preserving return position and front/paper/back depth.

Each preview shows the stored merchant, civil date, currency/total, review state and a short item excerpt. Paper uses an opaque surface, a small torn edge, hairline outline and two restrained shadows. Settings → Receipt paper offers **Match appearance** (the existing default: white in Light Mode, dark in Dark Mode) and **Always white** (white paper with dark text in both modes). The preference applies only to paper, including home previews, retained animation clones and full detail; surrounding chrome, swipe actions and wallet follow the system appearance. Content masks are applied before the shadow, so the silhouette casts a shadow beyond the preview’s bounds. The bottom wallet is a landscape cognac leather pocket inspired by the user’s MagSafe-wallet reference. A separate back panel behind every paper, a shallow scooped front mouth, raised welt, side/bottom stitching, sparse fixed grain and layered shadows distinguish it from a rounded card. The serif Wallet mark has a subtle pressed highlight. Light Mode uses lighter tan leather; Dark Mode uses deeper cognac. The paper is physically sandwiched between the back and front scene layers. Both wallet panels travel together during expansion, return and the final end spring; the paper retains its original depth between them. The material is drawn in SwiftUI rather than a photographic asset; native Glass controls retain the system appearance. Long previews fade toward the bottom; essential receipt summaries remain available to accessibility. The complete digital paper uses the same layout for every retailer and retains unknown values explicitly.

Opening a receipt keeps the stack mounted. Papers above the selected receipt move off the top; papers below it move off the bottom. A retained paper surface lifts from its measured preview rectangle into the reading position. The paper’s height grows to fit all contents, carrying its torn bottom edge with it. Returning restores the stack and scroll position with one shared spring. The selected paper keeps its original depth throughout: newer papers and the pocket are retained as foreground siblings, so they cover the selected paper as they return instead of changing its layer at completion. Source rectangles and rotations are frozen during the transition. Original evidence loads independently; editing is unavailable until that original is ready. Edit and source comparison retain the existing native review/evidence flows. Date review opens a native graphical [DatePicker](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/datepicker) in a sheet, with Cancel, Done and an explicit Clear date action. Opening or cancelling the selector never fills an unknown date. Done converts the chosen day to the stored Gregorian civil date in the local time zone; noon-based conversion is tested across DST and opposite time zones.

Reduce Motion replaces the travel animation with a short opacity change. Accessibility text sizes separate cards rather than overlap them. The hidden stack is removed from accessibility while a receipt is expanded. Native Liquid Glass navigation and action controls surround opaque reading surfaces.

A read-only comparison with Settings on the iOS 27 simulator showed scrolling content continuing beneath fixed controls with a progressive blur at the screen edges. The app uses Apple's [soft scroll edge effect](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/scrolledgeeffectstyle/soft), with full-screen scroll bounds and safe content margins, instead of a manually masked center frame. Home and expanded paper share this treatment; per-paper preview fades remain separate. The native effect changes with scrolling content and system controls, so it is not a permanently drawn gradient.

The implementation uses Apple's [animation completion](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/withanimation(_:completioncriteria:_:completion:)), [UIKit gesture integration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/uigesturerecognizerrepresentable), and [spring animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation/spring(response:dampingfraction:blendduration:)) APIs. This is a custom SwiftUI interaction inspired by Wallet, not a claim to reproduce Apple's private Wallet implementation.

## User-approved actions

- Archive removes a receipt from the home stack into Archive. Unarchive returns it to its chronological position.
- Star marks a favorite without changing chronology. Starred is available from Settings or the collection picker.
- Swipes reveal a [native glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffect(_:in:)) action and require a tap. Its width and height grow with drag distance, entirely within the revealed gap; the active row rises above neighboring papers while swiping. Deletion always needs a separate confirmation, with Cancel.
- Defaults: swipe left reveals Archive; swipe right reveals Star. Settings can assign Archive, Star, Delete or None independently to each direction.
- VoiceOver exposes named receipt actions without requiring horizontal gestures.

Organization is stored inside the authenticated encrypted receipt payload. Old documents lacking the optional organization field decode as unarchived/unstarred. Receipt edits retain organization; organization changes preserve original extraction, image, purchase timestamps and correction revisions. Swipe and paper-appearance preferences are encrypted in the existing metadata table with a separate authenticated context. Existing settings lacking paperAppearance retain both swipe choices and default to Match appearance; unsupported appearance values fail decoding instead of silently resetting preferences. No schema bump or plaintext receipt index is needed. Both write paths use the existing revocable transaction permit.

## Verification

The current elastic stack is recorded in [t05-elastic-stack](evidence/t05-elastic-stack/validation-summary.json). Earlier [leather pocket evidence](evidence/t05-leather-wallet/) remains unchanged. Exported screenshots and movies use the isolated fictional store; no personal originals or receipt fields are included.

The projection’s unit checks cover monotonic order, gap bounds, inverse mapping, peak travel speed and the anchored joint end spring across different viewport heights. A separate sample-addition test proves that exactly ten marked samples append without changing an existing receipt or settings and that a repeated addition adds zero duplicates. Native checks measure wider reading-zone gaps and different actual travel distances during the same slow swipe, reverse the swipe to check continuity, and open/return from the projected frame. Short-stack tests retain the back/paper/front pixel checks for one, two and three receipts. Existing swipe, archive/star, delete, date, paper appearance, 30-receipt navigation and native accessibility checks are rerun. Final verification covers thirteen unique native cases across two disjoint runs with identical final app sources, plus a repeated elastic case for animation capture. Fifty-one unique unit checks pass across the full suite and the separate sample-addition test; one physical-device check skips. Unit runs precede a UI-only immutable-render-snapshot fix, with projection/domain code unchanged. Three pre-existing review-screen invalid-frame runtime warnings remain, matching the preceding wallet run. Exact run scope and limits are in the manifest.

The requested demo data comprises ten clearly marked fictional No Frills, Costco and T&T receipts, dated across ten days and varying in item count and coupons. An explicit Debug simulator operation appends them through the normal encrypted store with source images and review fields. It skips matching source-image hashes, preserves current settings and never purges the normal store. The seed switch and sample generator are excluded from Release; the stored samples remain available after ordinary relaunch or installing Release. Ten were actually added to the normal Simulator wallet, and a second launch added zero while retaining all twelve records. The final verified Release is installed and normally launched.

Twelve existing narrow contrast exceptions remain documented, with strict structural checks and no broadened exception rules. Device accessibility, camera and physical file protection remain later iPhone checks. Recognition accuracy is not re-scored. User design review and T06 approval remain pending.
